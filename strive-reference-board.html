What the good ones have in common

Six patterns, and they are remarkably consistent.

01

Serif marks a practice. Sans marks a platform.

Four of the ten use a serif display, and they are the four closest to a personal practice: RewireMe, Alma (Tiempos), Two Chairs (Feijoa) and BetterUp (Ivar Display). The six running a sans are benefits platforms and education companies: Torch, Lyra, Modern Health, 80,000 Hours, Reforge and Spring Health. Typeface choice in this segment is a positioning signal, not a style preference.

02

Emphasis is carried by style, never by size

Two Chairs sets a single word in italic at 32px. Lyra sets its whole second line in italic at 52px, the same size as the first. Torch underlines two phrases at 62px. Not one of them makes the emphasised words bigger than the words around them.

03

One action, not two

The strongest heroes ask for a single thing: a ZIP code at Alma, a question at Modern Health, an email at 80,000 Hours. Two buttons of equal weight usually means the brief was never resolved.

04

Motion is singular and semantic

Where it exists it is one device, in the headline, saying something. BetterUp cycles three words inside its proposition. Torch rotates six categories and shows a 1/6 marker so you know it is a sequence. Nothing else on either screen moves.

05

Trust sits at the decision, not in a band further down

Insurer logos directly under the input at Alma. Coverage numbers directly under the button at Spring Health. Neither waits for a separate social-proof section two thousand pixels later.

06

Photography is the real differentiator, and the one thing that cannot be faked

Every site in Tier A runs on a single strong photograph. That is the largest upgrade available to Strive and the only one in this set that money reliably buys.

Tier A · Closest to Strive

A practice rather than a platform. Warm, premium, and running on one photograph.
Tier A

RewireMe Psychology

rewireme.com.au

Private psychology practice · Sydney · Awwwards-featured, Studio la Tas

Why it is here

The closest match in this whole set to Strive’s actual situation. A single practice rather than a platform, and it still reads as expensive. Useful proof that scale is not what makes a site feel premium.

What to steal

A full-bleed portrait, cropped tight and darkened, with a high-contrast Didone serif sitting in the empty space. A letterspaced “WELCOME TO” above it. Cream chrome, one near-black button, and restraint everywhere else.

Tier A

Alma

helloalma.com

Therapist matching · US

Why it is here

The warmest execution of serif-over-photography here, and the clearest single-action hero in the set. The entire first screen asks for one thing: a ZIP code.

What to steal

One input as the hero action instead of two competing buttons. And the insurer logos placed directly beneath it, so the trust signal sits at the decision rather than in a band further down the page.

Tier A

Two Chairs

twochairs.com

Mental health care · US

Why it is here

The strongest argument in the set that warm and premium are not opposites. Saturated yellow, serif italic for emphasis, and a hand-drawn rule under a single word.

What to steal

Italic doing the job a highlight colour would do. In the band under the hero, “The right care right when you need it” sets one word in italic at the same size as its neighbours. Measured, not eyeballed: 32px, font-style italic, no size change.

Tier B · Typographic and motion systems

Bigger companies, but the headline and motion patterns transfer directly.
Tier B

BetterUp

betterup.com

Coaching platform · US

Why it is here

The reference for purposeful motion. A stacked word list cycles inside the headline over cinematic video, and nothing else on the screen moves at all.

What to steal

One motion device, in the headline, doing semantic work. Three words cycle inside the proposition itself, set in Ivar Display at 110px, and nothing else on the screen moves. Compare that with sites where the motion is decoration bolted onto a static claim.

Tier B

Torch

torch.io

Leadership coaching · US

Why it is here

A rotating category rail with a visible 1/6 progress marker, sitting under a headline that underlines its key phrase rather than colouring it.

What to steal

The progress indicator. It tells the reader this is a sequence they can wait out rather than a surprise, which is most of what makes auto-rotation tolerable. The underline on “#1 leadership” and “coaching platform” is a real text-decoration at full headline size, not a highlight bar.

Tier B

Lyra Health

lyrahealth.com

Mental health benefits · US

Why it is here

Serif italic second line over a soft gradient. The friendliest point on the premium spectrum, and the closest tonally to where a career psychology practice should sit.

What to steal

A two-register headline: plain first line, italic second line, both at 52px. Hierarchy without a size jump, which keeps the block compact and stops the hero shouting. Notably it is set in a sans, Rethink Sans, which is why it reads friendly rather than formal.

Tier B

Modern Health

modernhealth.com

Mental health platform · US

Why it is here

Display type pinned to the left edge of a wide landscape photograph, with a select menu rather than a button as the primary action.

What to steal

“How can we help?” as a dropdown instead of a CTA. It turns a cold instruction into a question, which suits a practice far better than “Get started”.

Tier C · Credibility register

How to sound trustworthy to a research-minded audience without photography or claims.
Tier C

80,000 Hours · Career guide

80000hours.org/career-guide

Research-led career advice · UK

Why it is here

The closest audience match anywhere in this set: graduates heading into research and high-impact work. Visually plain, and completely credible to exactly the people Strive is targeting.

What to steal

The artefact as proof. A physical book sits beside the sign-up doing the work a testimonial would. Also the total absence of hype language, which is the register this audience actually trusts.

Note. Their homepage is currently running an AI-risk campaign rather than the usual career framing, so look at the career guide page rather than the root.

Tier C

Reforge

reforge.com

Professional education · US

Why it is here

Dark, typographic, and no imagery in the hero at all. Confidence expressed entirely through restraint.

What to steal

A hero that is words plus a logo row and nothing else. Worth knowing as the honest option when photography would have to be fabricated, which is precisely Strive’s position until there is a shoot.

Tier C

Spring Health

springhealth.com

Mental health benefits · US

Why it is here

Serif first line, accent-coloured second line, and the proof points stacked immediately under the button rather than banded off lower down.

What to steal

Vertical proof directly beneath the CTA. Three short lines, no icons, no card. It reads as fact rather than marketing furniture.

Applied to the prototype

Five changes, in order of how much they matter.

Commission a portrait shoot before launch

Highest impact

RewireMe and Alma both run on one photograph. Until Strive has one, the hero is competing with these sites one hand behind its back. Budget for a half-day with a real photographer, not stock.

Until the shoot, stay on the Reforge route

Already done

A graphic-led hero is the honest option when the alternative is a fabricated photograph. The interest map already in the prototype is doing that job and should stay.

Collapse the hero to one primary action

Quick change

The prototype currently runs “Book an intro conversation” against “See how it works” at equal weight. Every Tier A site here asks for one thing. Demote the second to a text link.

Test italic emphasis against the sapphire

Worth a test

Fraunces has a true italic. Setting “your research” in italic navy rather than sapphire would match what Lyra and Two Chairs actually do, and move the headline away from the tech register. The sapphire currently does the job a typeface should.

Keep the serif, and now for a measurable reason

Validated

The four serif sites in this set are the four that read as a practice rather than a platform. Fraunces puts Strive in that group on purpose. If anyone argues for a sans in review, this is the counter-argument.

Hold the proof slot empty rather than filling it

Deliberate

Spring Health and Alma both put proof directly under the CTA. Strive has none yet and should not invent any. Leave the slot designed and empty, and fill it the week there is something true to put in it.

Method, and the honest caveats

How this was put together

Twenty-four candidate sites were loaded in a real browser at 1440 by 900 and captured on 3 October 2026. Cookie banners and chat widgets were suppressed before capture so the hero could be seen. Only homepages and one guide page were reviewed; nothing deeper was assessed.

These are third-party websites, reproduced here as design references with links to the originals. None of them is affiliated with Strive Careers, and nothing here should be copied outright. What transfers is structure, typographic behaviour and restraint, not layout or artwork.

Live sites change. Several of these were running campaign heroes on the day rather than their standard framing, which is flagged where it matters. Re-check anything before presenting it as current.

Typeface names, font sizes, italics and underlines quoted throughout were read from each site’s live computed styles rather than judged by eye. An earlier draft of this board claimed that nine of the ten used a serif headline; the measurement showed four, which changed the conclusion materially and is why the first pattern reads the way it does.

No ranking, award or traffic data is claimed for any of these sites beyond the single Awwwards feature noted for RewireMe. The tiers are an editorial judgement about usefulness to this specific brief, not a measure of quality.
